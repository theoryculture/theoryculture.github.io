Graph Theory

A graph is a pair \( G = (V, E) \): a set of vertices and a set of edges joining them. The notes below are the first vocabulary.

Vertex and edge
Vertices are the points. An edge joins two vertices, its ends. The order is \( |V| \); the size is \( |E| \).
Simple graph and multigraph
A simple graph has no loops and no multiple edges. A multigraph may have several edges between the same pair of vertices.
Adjacent, incident, degree
Two vertices are adjacent if an edge joins them. The degree \( d(v) \) is the number of incident edges (a loop counts twice). Always \( \sum_v d(v) = 2|E| \). If every degree is \( r \), the graph is \( r \)-regular; 3-regular graphs are cubic.
Walk, trail, path, circuit, cycle
A walk is a sequence of edges. A trail repeats no edge; a path repeats no vertex. A circuit is a closed path. A cycle is a closed trail that enters and leaves a vertex at most once.
Connected and tree
The graph is connected if every pair of vertices is joined by a path. A tree is a connected graph with no cycle.
Complete, bipartite, complement
\( K_n \) has every possible edge. A bipartite graph splits \( V \) into two parts with all edges running between the parts. The complement keeps non-edges of \( G \) as edges.
Eulerian and Hamiltonian
Eulerian: a closed trail through every edge. A connected graph has one iff every degree is even. Hamiltonian: a circuit through every vertex. Uniquely Hamiltonian: exactly one such cycle.
Dominating cycle
Every vertex is on the cycle or adjacent to it: \( S \cup N(S) = V \).
Transition and compatibility
At a vertex, a transition pairs the entering and leaving edges of an Eulerian circuit. A cycle decomposition \( S \) is compatible with a trail \( T \) when \( X_T \cap X_S = \emptyset \).

Combinatorics

Combinatorics asks how many, whether any, and how they sit together. Counting, existence, and structure.

Binomial coefficient
\( \binom{n}{k} \) counts the \( k \)-subsets of an \( n \)-set. Pascal: \( \binom{n}{k} = \binom{n-1}{k} + \binom{n-1}{k-1} \).
Permutation and combination
Order matters for a permutation (\( n! \) of \( n \) objects). A combination forgets order: \( P(n,k) = k!\,\binom{n}{k} \).
Pigeonhole
\( n+1 \) pigeons, \( n \) holes: some hole holds at least two. Existence without a construction.
Double counting
Count one collection two ways. Handshaking is the graph case: \( \sum_v d(v) = 2|E| \).
Inclusion-exclusion
\( |A \cup B| = |A| + |B| - |A \cap B| \). Derangements are the permutation instance.
Mantel's theorem
A triangle-free graph on \( n \) vertices has at most \( \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor \) edges, with equality for the balanced complete bipartite graph.

Recreational Mathematics

Several of the central words in graph theory began as games: a walk through a city, a knight on a board, a dodecahedron you could hold.

Königsberg
Seven bridges, four odd-degree vertices. No walk crosses each bridge once. Euler's theorem: a connected graph is Eulerian iff every degree is even.
Knight's tour
Visit every square of the board by knight moves. That is a Hamiltonian path in the knight's graph; a closed tour is a Hamiltonian cycle.
Icosian game
Hamilton's 1857 game on the dodecahedron: a Hamiltonian cycle you can hold. Unlike the Eulerian case, there is no simple degree test.
One-stroke drawings
Zero odd vertices: start anywhere and close. Two: start at one odd vertex, end at the other. More than two: put the pencil down.
When it stops being a game
A single board becomes a class of graphs. Uniquely Hamiltonian means exactly one cycle through every vertex.
