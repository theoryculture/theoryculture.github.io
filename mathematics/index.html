Graph Theory and Combinatorics

A graph is a pair \( G = (V,E) \): a finite vertex set \( V \) together with a set \( E \) of edges joining pairs of vertices. In a simple graph the edges are distinct unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A multigraph may have several edges between the same pair. Loops are not allowed. Combinatorics asks how many finite configurations there are, whether any exist, and what structure they are forced to have; graphs are one of its languages.

Order, size, and degree
The order is \( |V| \); the size is \( |E| \). Two vertices are adjacent if an edge joins them. The degree \( d(v) \) is the number of edges incident with \( v \). Always \( \sum_v d(v) = 2|E| \). If every degree is \( r \), the graph is \( r \)-regular; 3-regular graphs are cubic.
Walk, trail, path, cycle
A walk is a sequence of vertices in which consecutive terms are adjacent. A trail is a walk with no repeated edge; a path is a walk with no repeated vertex. A walk is closed if it starts and ends at the same vertex. A cycle is a closed path of length at least 3 (in a simple graph); equivalently, a connected 2-regular subgraph.
Eulerian and Hamiltonian
An Eulerian circuit is a closed trail containing every edge; vertices may repeat. A connected graph has one if and only if every degree is even. A Hamiltonian cycle is a cycle containing every vertex. The graph is uniquely Hamiltonian if it has exactly one such cycle.
Connected and tree
The graph is connected if every pair of vertices is joined by a path. A tree is a connected graph with no cycle: equivalently, any two vertices are joined by a unique path.
Dominating cycle
A cycle \( C \) is dominating if every vertex not on \( C \) has a neighbour on \( C \). Writing \( S = V(C) \), this is \( S \cup N(S) = V \).
Transition and compatibility
At a vertex of even degree, a transition is a pairing of two incident edges: the edge used to enter and the edge used to leave along an Eulerian circuit \( T \). The set of all such pairings is the transition system \( X_T \). A cycle decomposition \( S \) — a partition of \( E \) into cycles — determines its own transition system \( X_S \). Then \( S \) is compatible with \( T \) when \( X_T \cap X_S = \emptyset \): the two edges of any transition of \( T \) lie on different cycles of \( S \).
Binomial coefficient
\( \binom{n}{k} \) is the number of \( k \)-element subsets of an \( n \)-element set, for integers \( 0 \le k \le n \). Pascal's identity: \( \binom{n}{k} = \binom{n-1}{k} + \binom{n-1}{k-1} \).
Pigeonhole principle
If \( n \) objects are placed in \( k \) boxes, then some box contains at least \( \lceil n/k \rceil \) objects. In particular, \( n+1 \) pigeons in \( n \) holes force a collision. The argument gives existence without a construction.
Inclusion-exclusion
\( |A \cup B| = |A| + |B| - |A \cap B| \). The same alternation of intersections counts the union of any finite family, and counts the derangements of \( n \) letters.
Mantel's theorem
A triangle-free graph on \( n \) vertices has at most \( \lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor \) edges, with equality if and only if the graph is the complete bipartite graph \( K_{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor, \lceil n/2 \rceil} \).

Recreational Mathematics

Recreational mathematics is often the first language of graph theory and combinatorics. The dedicated page has the essay A Marvellous Nonsense — Snarks, Martin Gardner, and Lewis Carroll — alongside a Poetry Talks Prague reading of The Hunting of the Snark on Štvanice, and eleven prompts from Scribbles and Giggles. The objects underneath are these.

Snark
A connected bridgeless cubic graph of chromatic index 4. Petersen is the smallest; the Blanuša snarks are the next. Gardner took the name from Carroll.
Pigeonhole
If \( n \) objects are placed in \( k \) boxes, some box contains at least \( \lceil n/k \rceil \) objects.
Eulerian trail
A trail containing every edge. A connected graph has a closed one if and only if every degree is even, and an open one if and only if exactly two vertices have odd degree.
Knight's tour
A Hamiltonian path in the knight's graph of the board; a closed tour is a Hamiltonian cycle.
Fibonacci
\( F_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n-2} \), with \( F_1 = F_2 = 1 \).
Palindrome
A sequence equal to its reverse.
