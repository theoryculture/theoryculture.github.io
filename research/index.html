Research

Graph theory · combinatorics · discrete mathematics

My research is in combinatorial graph theory, a branch of pure mathematics. The central objects are uniquely Hamiltonian graphs: graphs with exactly one cycle through every vertex. I am interested in constructions of such graphs, and in which classes of graphs cannot be uniquely Hamiltonian.

Uniquely Hamiltonian graphs

A graph is Hamiltonian if it has a cycle through every vertex. It is uniquely Hamiltonian if that cycle is the only one. The definitions — walk, trail, path, circuit, cycle, dominating cycle, transition system — are on the graph theory notes. Drawings are in the Graph Theory Art Gallery.

Sheehan's conjecture

Sheehan (1975) conjectured that there are no uniquely Hamiltonian \( r \)-regular simple graphs for \( r > 2 \). Equivalently: a simple connected regular graph is uniquely Hamiltonian only if it is 2-regular. The odd-regular case is settled (Thomason). The even-regular case remains open for \( 4 \le r \le 20 \). Thomassen proved there are no such graphs for sufficiently large degree; Haxell, Seamone, and Verstraëte brought the bound down to degree greater than 22.

Fleischner's construction

Fleischner (1994) gave a construction that starts from a cubic hypohamiltonian graph with a dominating cycle and aims at a uniquely Hamiltonian 4-regular multigraph. It works on the Petersen graph. In an attempt to produce an infinite family of uniquely Hamiltonian 4-regular graphs from cubic hypohamiltonian graphs, I showed that in general the construction does not hold for all such graphs. Counterexamples include the first and second Blanuša snarks. Open problems from this work are in preparation; see Publications.

The surrounding questions — compatible cycle decompositions, Sabidussi's conjecture, Eulerian circuits in (4,6)-regular multigraphs — sit between Hamiltonian uniqueness and the language of transitions recorded in the notes.

Computer science

Both of my degrees are in computer science. Hamiltonian cycles are a meeting point of the two subjects: existence and uniqueness are mathematical; deciding whether an arbitrary graph has such a cycle is NP-complete. Earlier work includes a game-theory thesis on traffic regulation and a literature survey of uniquely Hamiltonian graphs and the Hadwiger–Debrunner (\( p,q \))-problem. Details are on the portfolio.
