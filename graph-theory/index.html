Graph Theory

Walks · Eulerian circuits · Hamiltonian cycles

The vocabulary used for uniquely Hamiltonian graphs: how a graph is traversed, when a tour of the edges is Eulerian, when a tour of the vertices is Hamiltonian, and when that Hamiltonian cycle is unique. Graphs are finite. Loops are not allowed. Drawings of these objects are in the Graph Theory Art Gallery.

1. Graph, multigraph, subgraph
Graph
A graph \( G \) is a pair \( (V(G), E(G)) \), where \( V(G) \) is a finite non-empty set of vertices, and \( E(G) \) is a finite set of distinct unordered pairs of distinct elements of \( V(G) \), called edges. This is a simple graph: no loops, no multiple edges. \( V(G) \) is the vertex-set; \( E(G) \) is the edge-set. The order is \( |V(G)| \); the size is \( |E(G)| \).
Multigraph
\( G = (V, E) \) is a multigraph when \( E \) is a multiset of unordered pairs \( \{u,v\} \) with \( u \neq v \): the same pair may occur more than once. Loops are still forbidden.
Subgraph
If \( G = (V, E) \) is a graph, a subgraph \( H = (V_H, E_H) \) of \( G \) has \( V_H \subseteq V \) and \( E_H \subseteq E \), and each edge in \( E_H \) joins vertices in \( V_H \).
Induced and spanning
The induced subgraph \( G[S] \) on \( S \subseteq V \) keeps every edge of \( G \) with both ends in \( S \). A subgraph is spanning if it uses every vertex of \( G \).
Adjacent and incident
Two vertices are adjacent if an edge joins them. An edge is incident with its two ends.
2. Degree and regular graphs
Neighbourhood
The (open) neighbourhood \( N(v) \) of a vertex \( v \) is the set of vertices adjacent to \( v \). For \( S \subseteq V \), \( N(S) = \bigcup_{v \in S} N(v) \).
Degree (valency)
The degree \( d(v) \) (or valency) is the number of edges incident with \( v \). In a simple graph, \( d(v) = |N(v)| \). In a multigraph, multiple edges contribute separately. Always \( \sum_v d(v) = 2|E| \).
Regular graph
If every vertex of \( G \) has the same degree, then \( G \) is regular. If that common degree is \( r \), the graph is \( r \)-regular. A 3-regular graph is cubic.
\( (4,6) \)-regular
Every vertex has degree \( 4 \) or \( 6 \). These appear as the Eulerian multigraphs obtained by contracting the edges not on a dominating cycle of a cubic graph, in the construction of uniquely Hamiltonian 4-regular multigraphs.
3. Walk, trail, path, circuit, cycle

These are nested ways of traversing a graph. The distinctions matter: an Eulerian circuit may repeat vertices; a Hamiltonian cycle may not.

Walk
A walk of length \( r \) is a sequence of vertices \( v_0, v_1, \ldots, v_r \) such that \( v_{i-1}v_i \) is an edge for each \( i \). Equivalently, a sequence of edges \( v_0v_1,\, v_1v_2,\, \ldots,\, v_{r-1}v_r \). In a multigraph one specifies which edge is used.
Trail
A walk with no repeated edge.
Path
A walk with no repeated vertex.
Closed walk / closed trail
A walk or trail is closed if \( v_0 = v_r \).
Cycle
A cycle is a closed path of length at least 3 in a simple graph (length at least 2 if parallel edges are allowed). Equivalently: a connected 2-regular subgraph. The vertices \( v_0, \ldots, v_{r-1} \) are distinct, and the last edge returns to \( v_0 \).
Circuit
In older usage, a circuit is a closed path — the same object as a cycle. The word is kept in two frozen phrases: an Eulerian circuit is a closed trail through every edge (vertices may repeat); a Hamiltonian circuit is a Hamiltonian cycle. An Eulerian circuit is not, in general, a cycle.
Cycle graph \( C_n \)
The connected 2-regular graph on \( n \) vertices, \( n \ge 3 \). It is Eulerian and Hamiltonian; up to isomorphism it is the unique cycle of length \( n \).
4. Connected graphs
Connected
\( G \) is connected if there is a path joining each pair of vertices of \( G \).
Vertex-connectivity \( \kappa \)
\( \kappa(G) \) is the minimum number of vertices whose deletion disconnects \( G \), with the convention \( \kappa(K_n) = n-1 \). The graph is 2-connected if \( \kappa(G) \ge 2 \): equivalently, it has no cut-vertex.
Independent set
A set of vertices is independent if no two of them are adjacent. The largest size of such a set is the independence number \( \alpha(G) \).
Tree
A connected graph with no cycle. Equivalently: a connected graph on \( n \) vertices with \( n-1 \) edges; equivalently: any two vertices are joined by a unique path.
5. Eulerian trail, circuit, graph

An Eulerian graph is one that admits a closed trail containing every edge.

Eulerian trail
A trail that contains every edge of \( G \).
Eulerian circuit
A closed Eulerian trail: \( v_0 = v_r \), and every edge appears exactly once. Vertices of degree greater than 2 are visited more than once.
Eulerian graph
A connected graph with an Eulerian circuit.
Euler's theorem (1736), circuits
A connected graph has an Eulerian circuit if and only if every vertex has even degree.
Euler's theorem (1736), trails
A connected graph has an Eulerian trail if and only if it has exactly zero or two vertices of odd degree. (The number of odd-degree vertices is always even.) If there are two, every Eulerian trail starts at one of them and ends at the other. If there are none, every Eulerian trail is an Eulerian circuit.
Reducible Eulerian circuit
An Eulerian circuit that is a concatenation of two or more nonempty closed trails using the same transitions as \( T \) — a disjoint union of smaller Eulerian circuits whose transitions agree with those of \( T \).
Irreducible Eulerian circuit
An Eulerian circuit of \( G - E(S_0) \) whose transitions agree with those of \( T \) except on the edges of the cycles in \( S_0 \), and which cannot be decomposed into a union of smaller nonempty Eulerian circuits.
6. Hamiltonian and uniquely Hamiltonian
Hamiltonian cycle
A cycle that contains every vertex of \( G \). A graph with such a cycle is Hamiltonian. (Older papers say Hamiltonian circuit for the same object.)
Uniquely Hamiltonian graph
A graph with exactly one Hamiltonian cycle, as a subgraph: two Hamiltonian cycles are the same when they have the same edge set.
Sheehan's conjecture (1975)
If \( G \) is a finite simple \( r \)-regular graph with \( r > 2 \), then \( G \) is not uniquely Hamiltonian. Equivalently: a simple connected regular graph is uniquely Hamiltonian only if it is 2-regular. The odd-regular case is settled (Thomason). There are no such graphs for \( r > 22 \) (Haxell–Seamone–Verstraëte). The even-regular case remains open for \( 4 \le r \le 22 \).
Hypohamiltonian
Non-Hamiltonian, but \( G - v \) is Hamiltonian for every vertex \( v \). Fleischner's construction starts from a cubic hypohamiltonian graph with a dominating cycle; Petersen is the usual example.
7. Dominating cycle
Dominating cycle
A cycle \( C \) is dominating if every vertex of \( G \) is either on \( C \) or adjacent to a vertex of \( C \). If \( S = V(C) \), then \( S \cup N(S) = V(G) \).
Maximal unique dominating cycle
In the construction, uniqueness of a Hamiltonian cycle in the 4-regular graph is obtained from uniqueness of a maximal dominating cycle in an associated cubic graph \( G_3 \).
8. Transitions and half-edges

An Eulerian circuit determines, at each vertex, a pairing of the incident edges: the edge used to enter and the edge used to leave, on each visit.

Transition
At a vertex \( v \) of even degree, a pair of incident edges grouped as enter/leave. The set of transitions at \( v \) is written \( X(v) \). There are \( d(v)/2 \) of them.
Half-edge
Each of the two edges in a transition is called a half-edge in this literature: two half-edges make one transition.
Transition system
For every \( v \in V(G) \), let \( X(v) \) be a partition of the edges incident with \( v \) into unordered pairs. Each pair is a transition. The union \[ X = \bigcup_{v \in V(G)} X(v) \] is a (complete) system of transitions. The transition system of an Eulerian circuit \( T \) is written \( X_T \).
9. Cycle decomposition
Cycle cover
A system of cycles \( S = \{C_1, \ldots, C_m\} \) in a multigraph \( H \) is a cycle cover of \( H \) if every edge of \( H \) lies on at least one cycle of \( S \).
Cycle decomposition
A set of cycles that partition \( E(H) \): they are edge-disjoint and cover every edge exactly once. A graph admits a cycle decomposition if and only if every degree is even (Veblen). In particular every Eulerian graph has one. The empty set is a cycle decomposition of a graph with no edges. Writing \( S = \{C_1, \ldots, C_k\} \) for such a set, the associated transition system is \( X_S \): consecutive edges of a cycle of \( S \) give a pair of half-edges as a transition.
10. Compatibility

The relation between a cycle decomposition and the transitions of an Eulerian circuit. This is the language of Sabidussi's conjecture.

Compatible transition systems
Two systems of transitions \( X' \) and \( X'' \) are compatible if \( X' \cap X'' = \emptyset \).
Compatibility of \( S \) with \( T \)
Let \( T \) be an Eulerian trail and \( S \) a cycle decomposition.
  • \( X_T \) is the system of transitions that describes how the trail moves from one edge to another at each vertex.
  • \( X_S \) is the system of transitions that describes how the cycles of \( S \) pair edges at each vertex.
  • \( S \) is compatible with \( T \) if \( X_T \cap X_S = \emptyset \): the two edges of any transition of \( T \) lie on different cycles of \( S \).
Sabidussi's conjecture (1975)
For every connected Eulerian graph \( G \) of minimum degree at least 4, and every Eulerian circuit \( T \) of \( G \), there exists a cycle decomposition \( S \) compatible with \( T \).
\( S_0 \)
A set of pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles, each compatible with \( T \), such that \( G - E(S_0) \) inherits from \( T \) an irreducible Eulerian circuit \( T_0 \). If there is only one such set, Fleischner's construction yields a uniquely Hamiltonian 4-regular multigraph.
11. Automorphisms
Graph automorphism
An automorphism of \( G \) is a bijection \( \varphi \colon V(G) \to V(G) \) such that, for all \( u, v \in V(G) \), \[ uv \in E(G) \iff \varphi(u)\varphi(v) \in E(G). \] Thus \( \varphi \) preserves adjacency. The set of all automorphisms is a group, written \( \mathrm{Aut}(G) \).
Hamiltonian cycle under automorphism
If \( C \) is a Hamiltonian cycle and \( \varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}(G) \), the image \( \varphi(C) \) is a Hamiltonian cycle with edge set \( \{ \varphi(u)\varphi(v) : uv \in E(C) \} \). Two Hamiltonian cycles may lie in the same \( \mathrm{Aut}(G) \)-orbit without being equal as edge sets. Uniquely Hamiltonian means exactly one Hamiltonian cycle as a subgraph, not uniqueness up to automorphism.
12. Special graphs
Complete graph \( K_n \)
Every pair of distinct vertices is joined by exactly one edge. \( K_n \) is \( (n-1) \)-regular and has \( \binom{n}{2} \) edges.
Path graph \( P_n \)
A path on \( n \) vertices (\( n-1 \) edges).
Bipartite graph
The vertex set splits as \( V = A \cup B \) with \( A \cap B = \emptyset \), and every edge has one end in \( A \) and one end in \( B \). Equivalently, \( G \) contains no odd cycle.
Complete bipartite graph \( K_{s,t} \)
Every vertex of a part of size \( s \) is joined to every vertex of a part of size \( t \). The graph \( K_{1,n} \) is a star.
Complement \( \overline{G} \)
Same vertex set as \( G \); two distinct vertices are adjacent in \( \overline{G} \) if and only if they are not adjacent in \( G \). (Defined for simple graphs.)
Isomorphism
A bijection \( f \colon V(G) \to V(H) \) such that \( \{u,v\} \) is an edge of \( G \) if and only if \( \{f(u), f(v)\} \) is an edge of \( H \). Then \( G \) and \( H \) are isomorphic, written \( G \cong H \). An automorphism is an isomorphism from \( G \) to itself.
