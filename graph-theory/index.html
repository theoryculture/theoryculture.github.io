Graph Theory

Definitions

Graph theory is the mathematics of vertices and edges. The vocabulary below is the one used in the uniquely Hamiltonian notes: how a graph is traversed, when a tour of the edges is Eulerian, when a tour of the vertices is Hamiltonian, and when that Hamiltonian cycle is the only one. Graphs are finite. Loops are not allowed. This is pure mathematics; the algorithmic twin is computer science. Drawings of these objects are in the Graph Theory Art Gallery.

1. Graph, multigraph, subgraph
Graph
A graph \( G \) is a pair \( (V(G), E(G)) \), where \( V(G) \) is a finite non-empty set of elements called vertices, and \( E(G) \) is a finite set of distinct unordered pairs of distinct elements of \( V(G) \), called edges. \( V(G) \) is the vertex-set of \( G \); \( E(G) \) is the edge-set.
Multigraph
\( G = (V, E) \) is a multigraph when \( E \) is a multiset of pairs from \( V \times V \): the pair \( \{u,v\} \) with \( u \neq v \) may occur more than once. To avoid loops, \( u \neq v \) in every pair.
Subgraph
If \( G = (V, E) \) is a graph, a subgraph \( H = (V_H, E_H) \) of \( G \) has \( V_H \subseteq V \) and \( E_H \subseteq E \), and each edge in \( E_H \) joins vertices in \( V_H \).
Induced and spanning
The induced subgraph \( G[S] \) on \( S \subseteq V \) keeps every edge of \( G \) with both ends in \( S \). A subgraph is spanning if it uses every vertex of \( G \).
Adjacent and incident
Two vertices are adjacent if an edge joins them. An edge is incident with its two ends.
2. Degree and regular graphs
Neighbourhood
The neighbourhood \( N(v) \) of a vertex \( v \) is the set of vertices adjacent to \( v \).
Degree (valency)
The degree \( d(v) \) (or valency) is the number of edges incident with \( v \). In a simple graph, \( d(v) = |N(v)| \). Always \( \sum_v d(v) = 2|E| \).
Regular graph
If all vertices of \( G \) have the same valency, then \( G \) is regular. If that common degree is \( r \), the graph is \( r \)-regular. A 3-regular graph is cubic.
\( (4,6) \)-regular
Every vertex has degree \( 4 \) or \( 6 \). These appear as the Eulerian multigraphs in the construction of uniquely Hamiltonian 4-regular multigraphs.
3. Walk, trail, path, circuit, cycle
Walk
A sequence of edges of the form \( v_0v_1,\, v_1v_2,\, \ldots,\, v_{r-1}v_r \) (sometimes written \( v_0, v_1, \ldots, v_r \)) is a walk of length \( r \) from \( v_0 \) to \( v_r \).
Trail
If all the edges in a walk are distinct, the walk is a trail.
Path
If the vertices \( v_0, \ldots, v_r \) in a walk are all distinct, the walk is a path.
Closed walk / closed trail
A walk or trail is closed if the start vertex and the end vertex are the same: \( v_0 = v_r \).
Circuit
A path in which \( v_0, \ldots, v_r \) are all distinct except \( v_0 = v_r \) is a circuit (a closed path).
Cycle
A closed trail that enters and leaves a vertex at most once. Both Hamiltonian cycles and Eulerian circuits are special cases of this way of traversing a graph.
Cycle graph \( C_n \)
A graph all of whose edges are traversed by a cycle of length \( n \). All cycle graphs are Eulerian, Hamiltonian, connected, and 2-regular.
4. Connected graphs
Connected
\( G \) is connected if there is a path joining each pair of vertices of \( G \).
Vertex-connectivity \( \kappa \)
\( \kappa(G) \) is the minimum number of vertices that must be removed to disconnect \( G \). The graph is 2-connected if \( \kappa(G) \ge 2 \).
Independent set
A set of vertices in \( G \) is independent if no two of them are adjacent. The largest number of vertices in such a set is the independence number \( \beta(G) \).
Tree
A connected graph with no cycle. Equivalently: a connected graph on \( n \) vertices with \( n-1 \) edges; equivalently: any two vertices are joined by a unique path.
5. Eulerian trail, circuit, graph

An Eulerian graph is one whose edges can be traversed without lifting the pen: a closed trail through every edge.

Eulerian trail
A connected graph \( G \) has an Eulerian trail if it has a trail which includes every edge of \( E(G) \).
Eulerian circuit
An Eulerian trail that is closed, \( v_0 = v_r \). Then the trail starts and finishes at the same vertex.
Eulerian graph
A connected graph with an Eulerian circuit.
Euler's theorem (1736), circuits
A connected graph has an Eulerian circuit if and only if every vertex has even degree.
Euler's theorem (1736), trails
A connected graph has an Eulerian trail if and only if it has exactly zero or two vertices of odd degree. If there are two, every Eulerian trail starts at one of them and ends at the other. If there are none, every Eulerian trail is an Eulerian circuit.
Reducible Eulerian circuit
A disjoint union of smaller Eulerian circuits (traversing subgraphs) whose transitions are the same as those of \( T \).
Irreducible Eulerian circuit
An Eulerian circuit of \( G - E(S_0) \) whose transitions agree with those of \( T \) (except on edges of the cycles in \( S_0 \)), and which cannot be decomposed into a union of smaller non-empty Eulerian circuits.
6. Hamiltonian and uniquely Hamiltonian
Hamiltonian cycle (Hamiltonian circuit)
A circuit which includes every vertex of \( V(G) \) exactly once and returns to the start. A graph with such a circuit is Hamiltonian.
Uniquely Hamiltonian graph
A graph with exactly one Hamiltonian cycle.
Sheehan's conjecture (1975)
If \( G \) is a finite simple \( r \)-regular graph with \( r > 2 \), then \( G \) is not uniquely Hamiltonian. Equivalently, as a necessary condition: a simple connected regular graph is uniquely Hamiltonian only if it is 2-regular. The odd-regular case is settled (Thomason). The even-regular case remains open for \( 4 \le r \le 20 \).
Hypohamiltonian
Non-Hamiltonian, but \( G - v \) is Hamiltonian for every vertex \( v \). The construction in the notes starts from a cubic hypohamiltonian graph with a dominating cycle missing one vertex; Petersen is the usual example.
7. Dominating cycle
Dominating cycle
A cycle is dominating when every vertex of the graph is either on the cycle or adjacent to a vertex on the cycle. If \( S \) is the vertex set of the cycle, then \( S \cup N(S) = V \).
Maximal unique dominating cycle
In the construction, uniqueness of a Hamiltonian cycle in the 4-regular graph is obtained from uniqueness of a maximal dominating cycle in an associated cubic graph \( G_3 \).
8. Transitions and half-edges

An Eulerian circuit gives, at each vertex, a pairing of the incident edges: the edge used to enter and the edge used to leave.

Transition
At a vertex \( v \), a pair of incident edges grouped together as enter/leave. The set of transitions at \( v \) is written \( X(v) \).
Half-edge
Each edge in a transition is a half-edge: two half-edges make one transition.
Transition system
For every \( v \in V(G) \), let \( X(v) \) denote a partition of the edges incident to \( v \) into classes of two elements. Each class is a transition. The union \[ X = \bigcup_{v \in V(G)} X(v) \] is a (complete) system of transitions. The transition system of an Eulerian circuit \( T \) is written \( X_T \).
9. Cycle decomposition
Cycle cover
A system of cycles \( S = \{C_1, \ldots, C_m\} \) in a multigraph \( H \) is a cycle cover of \( H \) if the union of the cycles covers every edge of \( H \).
Cycle decomposition
A set of cycles in \( H \) that do not share any edges and that cover every edge of \( H \) exactly once. Equivalently: a partition of \( E(H) \) into cycles. A graph with a cycle decomposition is necessarily Eulerian. The empty set is a cycle decomposition of a graph with no edges. Writing \( S = \{C_1, \ldots, C_k\} \) for such a set, the associated transition system is \( X_S \): consecutive edges of a cycle give a pair of half-edges as a transition.
10. Compatibility

The relation between a cycle decomposition and the transitions of an Eulerian circuit. This is the language of Sabidussi's conjecture.

Compatible transition systems
Two systems of transitions \( X' \) and \( X'' \) are compatible if \( X' \cap X'' = \emptyset \).
Compatibility of \( S \) with \( T \)
Let \( T \) be an Eulerian trail and \( S \) a cycle decomposition.
  • \( X_T \) is the system of transitions that describes how the trail moves from one edge to another at each vertex.
  • \( X_S \) is the system of transitions that describes how the cycle decomposition pairs edges at each vertex.
  • \( S \) is compatible with \( T \) if \( X_T \) and \( X_S \) are disjoint: the two half-edges of a transition of \( X_T \) belong to different cycles of \( S \). In symbols, \( X_T \cap X_S = \emptyset \).
Sabidussi's conjecture (1975)
For a connected Eulerian graph \( G \) of minimum degree 4 with an Eulerian circuit \( T \), there exists a cycle decomposition \( S \) compatible with \( T \).
\( S_0 \)
A set of pairwise vertex-disjoint compatible cycles from a cycle decomposition of \( G \) such that \( G - E(S_0) \) induces an irreducible Eulerian circuit \( T_0 \). If there is only one such \( S_0 \), the construction yields a uniquely Hamiltonian 4-regular multigraph.
11. Automorphisms
Graph automorphism
For a graph \( G(V,E) \), an automorphism is a bijection \( \varphi \colon V(G) \to V(G) \) such that, for all \( u, v \in V \), \[ uv \in E(G) \iff \varphi(u)\varphi(v) \in E(G). \] In other words, \( \varphi \) maps vertices of \( G \) to vertices of \( G \) and preserves the edge structure. \( \mathrm{Aut}(G) \) is the set of all automorphisms of \( G \).
Hamiltonian cycle under automorphism
If \( C \) is a Hamiltonian cycle and \( \varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}(G) \), the image cycle \( \varphi(C) \) has edge set \( \{ \varphi(u)\varphi(v) : uv \in E(C) \} \). Two Hamiltonian cycles may be the same up to automorphism without being the same set of edges. Uniquely Hamiltonian still means exactly one Hamiltonian cycle as a set of edges.
12. Special graphs
Complete graph \( K_n \)
Every pair of distinct vertices is joined by exactly one edge. \( K_n \) is \( (n-1) \)-regular and has \( \binom{n}{2} \) edges.
Path graph \( P_n \)
A path on \( n \) vertices.
Bipartite graph
The vertex set splits as \( V = A \cup B \), \( A \cap B = \emptyset \), and every edge has one end in \( A \) and one end in \( B \). Equivalently, \( G \) contains no odd cycle.
Complete bipartite graph \( K_{s,t} \)
Every vertex of a part of size \( s \) is joined to every vertex of a part of size \( t \). The graph \( K_{1,n} \) is a star.
Complement \( \overline{G} \)
Same vertex set as \( G \); two vertices are adjacent in \( \overline{G} \) if and only if they are not adjacent in \( G \).
Isomorphism
A bijection \( f \colon V(G) \to V(H) \) such that \( \{u,v\} \) is an edge of \( G \) if and only if \( \{f(u), f(v)\} \) is an edge of \( H \). Then \( G \) and \( H \) are the same graph, drawn differently. An automorphism is an isomorphism from \( G \) to itself.
